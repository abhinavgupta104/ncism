"The science of life" — ayur, life; veda, science.

The science of life

Ayurveda means "the science of life" — ayur means "life" and veda means "science" in Sanskrit. It is a discipline of the upaveda, or "auxiliary knowledge", within the Vedic tradition. Ayurveda has its prime origin from the Atharva-Veda, and stands apart as a supplement of the Rig-Veda. Dhanvantari is worshipped as the god of Ayurveda.

The aim of this system is to prevent illness, heal the sick and preserve life. Ayurveda has its origins in India and has extended its wings into various parts of the world. It was taught in the Gurukula system in ancient days, which has now evolved into undergraduate and postgraduate courses offered by institutions.

Tridosha — the three humours

Ayurvedic physiology rests on the tridosha: three functional principles whose balance governs health and whose disturbance produces disease. Each is composed of the panchamahabhuta, the five great elements.

Diagnosis begins with prakriti — the constitution a person is born with — read against vikriti, their present state of imbalance. Treatment then follows through diet, regimen, medicine and the purificatory procedures of Panchakarma.

वात

Vata

Air + Ether · Vayu, Akasha

The principle of movement — respiration, circulation, nerve impulse and elimination. Governs all motion in the body and mind.

पित्त

Pitta

Fire + Water · Agni, Jala

The principle of transformation — digestion, metabolism, body temperature, vision and comprehension.

कफ

Kapha

Water + Earth · Jala, Prithvi

The principle of cohesion and structure — lubrication, stability, immunity and the substance of the tissues.

Ashtanga Ayurveda — the eight limbs

The classical corpus divides Ayurveda into eight clinical branches. They remain the organising spine of the modern BAMS curriculum and of postgraduate specialisation.

Sessions & institutions

The Commission publishes an academic calendar for every BAMS batch, fixing admission cut-off dates, term dates and examination windows. Permission status for each college is notified separately for every academic year.

Students should confirm a college's permission status for the relevant academic year before accepting admission — permission is granted year by year, not once.
