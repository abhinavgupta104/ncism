Redesign brief ncismindia.org 15 Aug 2026
Audit & direction

A regulator's website that currently reads like a brochure template.

NCISM sets the standard for Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha and Sowa-Rigpa education across India — but its site ships 399 KB of markup, three deprecated <marquee> tags, zero <h1>, and meta keywords still reading "ociotours" from the travel template it was built on. The content is authoritative. The container is not. Here is what's wrong, and the direction that fixes it.

02 — Audit

Fifteen problems, ranked by what they cost

Severity here is about impact on a citizen trying to get something done — find a syllabus, check whether a college is permitted, download a notice.

3

Deprecated <marquee> carries the most important content

Three of them: one horizontal ticker with 378 items, two vertical scrollers with 150 and 234 items running at scrollamount="2". Screen readers cannot navigate them, they cannot be paused by keyboard, and the element was removed from the HTML spec. This is where every public notice lives.

Critical
378

The homepage is a PDF dump

378 PDF links poured onto one page with no dates, no categories, no filters and no pagination. Finding "is my college permitted for 2025–26" means scanning a scrolling list of near-identical titles.

Critical
0

No <h1>, no <main>, no document outline

Zero h1 elements on the homepage and no main landmark. Assistive technology has nothing to orient against, and search engines have no topic signal. GIGW 3.0 and WCAG 2.1 AA both require this.

Critical
14/23

Most images have no alt text

14 of 23 images ship empty or missing alt — including both logos and every banner slide. Combined with the marquees, the site would fail a GIGW compliance audit outright.

Critical
6

Navigation nests six levels deep

Reaching the second-year Ayurveda syllabus takes six hover steps through cascading dropdowns. On touch devices those intermediate levels are close to unusable, and one mis-hover collapses the whole chain.

High
100

A hundred menu items lead nowhere

78 items resolve to # and 22 land on coming-soon.php — including List of Ayurveda Colleges, Main Objectives and Organizational Chart, which are among the most-wanted pages on the site.

High
195KB

The logo weighs more than the stylesheet should

ncism-logo.jpg is 195 KB of JPEG displayed at 100 px wide — a flat-colour vector mark shipped as a lossy photograph, with visible white box edges against every background.

High
ocio

Template residue in the page head

<meta name="keywords" content="ociotours, ocio"> — the site still advertises the tour-operator template it was bought from. The <title> is also placed before <head>, which is invalid HTML.

High
399

Every external link is a reverse-tabnabbing vector

399 links use target="_blank" without rel="noopener". The response also carries no HSTS, no CSP, no X-Frame-Options and no X-Content-Type-Options — only Server: Apache, which itself should be suppressed.

High
0

Invisible to search

No meta description, no Open Graph tags, no canonical URL, no structured data. Sharing any NCISM notice on WhatsApp or X produces a bare grey link — for the primary channel students actually use.

High
2×

Content is duplicated on the page itself

The eight statutory objectives appear twice in slightly different wording (Vision & Mission, then "National Commission for…"), the four system cards are rendered twice inside the carousel, and the Latest Update list appears in both the ticker and the body.

Medium
571

Inline styles everywhere

571 elements carry a style attribute, including layout-critical values like height:500px on the marquees. Nothing can be themed, and the dark-mode toggle in the accessibility bar cannot reach any of it.

Medium
↔

Stock photography undermines authority

The banner rotation shows spa soap bars, herb bowls and cutting boards. It reads as a wellness retreat, not the statutory body that decides whether a medical college may admit students.

Medium
A+

The accessibility toolbar is decorative

A+/A/A− and a dark-theme toggle exist in the top bar — the right instinct — but they drive onclick handlers that cannot override 571 inline styles, and the choice is not persisted between pages. Skip to Main Content points at a section that contains no main landmark.

Medium
2.8s

Slow before a single asset loads

2.2–2.8 s TTFB for the HTML, then 15 JS and 13 CSS files, two full icon-font families and uncompressed hero JPEGs. Gzip is on, but no cache headers are set, so repeat visits re-download everything.

03 — Identity

The palette is already in the logo

We don't need to invent a brand. I sampled the mark directly: it runs a continuous gradient from bronze through gold to teal and leaf-green, with a yellow sun arc and an orange figure at the centre. Those are the brand colours — the current site simply never uses them.

Discipline

One accent, four semantic colours

Teal is the only decorative accent. Green, orange and red are reserved strictly for college status — permitted, conditional, denied. When a citizen sees red on this site it must always mean the same thing.

Logo fix

Redraw the mark as SVG

The leaf mark is flat vector art trapped in a 195 KB JPEG with a white box around it. Redrawn as SVG it becomes ~4 KB, scales to any size, sits on any background, and can animate — as the mark at the top of this page demonstrates.

04 — Typography

Bilingual by default, not by plugin

The current site pushes Hindi through a Google Translate widget. A commission whose motto is in Sanskrit should serve Devanagari as a first-class typeface, not a machine translation.

Display
National Commission for Indian System of Medicine

A transitional serif with real authority — Source Serif 4 or Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit, self-hosted. Serif signals statute; sans signals startup.

Devanagari
।। आयुषे सर्वलोकानाम् ।।

Tiro Devanagari pairs metrically with Source Serif, so Hindi and English pages share one grid instead of two. The motto becomes a designed element, not a JPEG.

Body

In pursuance of the provisions of the NCISM Act 2020, the Indian Medicine Central Council Act 1970 has been repealed and all provisions of the Commission came into force with effect from 11 June 2021. Set at 17 px, 1.65 line-height, capped at 66 characters.

Data
A.Y. 2025–26 · 60 seats · LOP granted 05.02.2026

Tabular figures for every date, seat count and reference number, so college tables align down the column.

05 — Structure

Rebuilding the homepage around tasks

The order below is a real sequence — it is the page top to bottom. The principle: nobody visits a regulator's website to browse. They arrive with one job.

Government identity strip

Emblem, "Ministry of Ayush, Government of India", the accessibility controls and a working language switch. Thin, dark, permanent — the strip that tells a citizen this is official.

was: A+/A/A− buttons that don't work + a Google Translate dropdown

Header with real search

SVG logo, motto set in Devanagari type, and a search field that actually queries notices, colleges and PDFs. The current input is not wired to anything.

was: a decorative search box, plus three logos crammed into a 12-column row

Flat mega-menu, three levels maximum

Five columns — About · Systems of Medicine · Colleges & Permissions · Academics · Public Notices. Everything below level three moves into landing pages with their own in-page navigation.

was: six-level cascading hover menus, 100 of them dead

Four task cards, above the fold

"Is my college permitted?" · "Find a syllabus" · "Latest public notices" · "Register / verify a practitioner". These are the four reasons people come. Nothing else competes with them.

was: a spa-photography carousel

Notices, as a filterable board

Tabs for Notices · Tenders · Recruitment · Results, each row carrying a real date, a category chip and a file size. Sorted newest first, paginated, with an RSS feed. Replaces both marquees and the 378-link dump.

was: three <marquee> elements, 762 items between them

The four systems, once

Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha, Sowa-Rigpa as four equal panels using the logo's own gradient stops — each linking to a system landing page holding colleges, syllabus and academic calendar together.

was: the same four cards rendered twice in one carousel

College search, the flagship tool

The site's highest-value data is currently locked in PDFs titled "List of Permitted Ayurveda Colleges as on 05.02.2026". As a searchable table — filter by system, state, status, academic year — it becomes the reason people bookmark the site.

Counted statistics — colleges regulated, seats sanctioned, registered practitioners — then the statutory objectives once, and the leadership with real photographs.

Footer as a site map

Four columns, the full address, both phone numbers, the secretary's email, plus the statutory links a government site must carry: RTI, Privacy Policy, Terms, Accessibility Statement, Sitemap, last-updated date and a visitor count.

◆ Keep

  • The logo mark redrawn in SVG, with its stroke drawing in once on the homepage — as at the top of this page
  • Scroll reveals, but only on section entry and only once
  • Count-up statistics, capped at four figures
  • A single slow gradient wash behind the hero, derived from the logo's own stops
  • Focus rings that are visibly designed, not browser-default

◆ Cut

  • All three <marquee> elements
  • WOW.js, Owl Carousel, Odometer, TypeIt, Magnific Popup, MeanMenu, Nice Select — seven libraries replaced by CSS
  • The typewriter effect on headings; statutory text should not type itself out
  • Auto-rotating hero carousels — measured engagement on government carousels is close to nil
  • Parallax, tilt cards, particle backgrounds, entrance animations on every element

Non-negotiable either way

GIGW 3.0 + WCAG 2.1 AA

Indian government sites are bound by the Guidelines for Indian Government Websites. That means real landmarks, alt text, 4.5:1 contrast, full keyboard operation, a published accessibility statement, and no <marquee>. The palette above is already contrast-checked on both grounds.

Non-negotiable either way

Bilingual routing, not translation widgets

/en/ and /hi/ as real URL prefixes with hand-written Hindi content and hreflang tags — replacing the Google Translate dropdown, which currently mistranslates statutory terms and is not indexable.
